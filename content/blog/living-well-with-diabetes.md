One in twenty Kenyan adults is living with diabetes — and half of them do not know it yet. The good news I share with every newly diagnosed patient is this: **diabetes is very manageable**. With a steady routine you can live a long, full, active life. Here is the practical guide I give at our chronic care clinic.

## First, know your numbers

You cannot manage what you do not measure:

- **Fasting blood sugar:** 4.4 – 7.0 mmol/L for most adults
- **HbA1c (3-month average):** below 7% — test at least twice a year
- **Blood pressure:** below 140/90 mmHg
- **Foot and eye checks:** once a year, every year, without fail

A home glucometer costs less than a month of unhealthy takeaways. Test fasting 2–3 mornings a week and keep a simple record on your phone or notebook — bring it to your reviews.

## Food: your most powerful daily medicine

You do not need special "diabetic products". You need better versions of the Kenyan food you already love:

- Swap white ugali for **millet or sorghum ugali**, and reduce the portion to the size of your fist.
- Load half your plate with sukuma wiki, cabbage, managu or steamed vegetables.
- Choose ndengu, beans and fish more often; cut processed meats.
- Kill the sugar habit in tea gradually — halve it each month. Your taste buds will adjust, I promise.
- Never skip meals then "compensate" — big swings are the enemy of stable sugar.

## Medication rhythm matters

Whether you are on metformin, other tablets, or insulin — take them **at the same time every day**. Do not stop because you "feel fine"; diabetes does its damage quietly. If side effects bother you or the cost is heavy, tell us — there are always alternatives. Our pharmacy sends **refill reminders by WhatsApp** so you never run dry.

## Move, sleep, and guard your feet

Thirty minutes of brisk walking — to the matatu stage and back counts — five days a week lowers blood sugar as effectively as some tablets. Check your feet daily for cuts, blisters or colour changes; wash and dry well between the toes, and never walk barefoot. Poorly healing foot wounds are how diabetes turns dangerous, fast.

> A patient of mine in his 60s has lived with diabetes for 22 years. His HbA1c is better than most people without diabetes. His secret? "I test, I walk, I never miss my clinic." That is the whole formula.
