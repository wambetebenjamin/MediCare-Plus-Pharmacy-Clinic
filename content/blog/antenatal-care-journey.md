Congratulations, you are pregnant. Whether this is your first baby or your fourth, antenatal care (ANC) is the single most important investment you can make in your baby's health and your own. In Kenya we recommend **at least eight ANC contacts** during pregnancy. Let me walk you through what actually happens at each stage, the way I explain it to mothers at our clinics.

## First trimester (weeks 1–13): confirm and start strong

Your first visit confirms the pregnancy and sets your baseline. Expect a dating scan or referral for one, blood tests (blood group, haemoglobin, HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B), a urine test and your booking profile. This is when we start **folic acid**, crucial for your baby's brain and spine development in these early weeks. Morning sickness is normal; eat small, dry snacks and sip fluids often.

> Do not wait to "feel pregnant enough" to book. Mothers who start ANC before 12 weeks have healthier pregnancies, so come as soon as you suspect you might be expecting.

## Second trimester (weeks 14–27): the growth phase

Energy usually returns now. Between **weeks 18 and 22** you will have your anomaly scan, the detailed ultrasound that checks baby's organs. We monitor your blood pressure and urine at every visit to catch pre-eclampsia early, check baby's heartbeat, and start iron supplementation to prevent anaemia, which is very common in Kenyan pregnancies. This is also a good time to sign up for antenatal classes and begin childbirth planning.

## Third trimester (weeks 28–40): prepare for delivery

Visits become more frequent: every two weeks, then weekly. We track baby's position and growth, give tetanus-diphtheria doses if due, and finalise your **birth plan**: where you will deliver, who accompanies you, and how you will get there fast. Pack your hospital bag by week 36.

### Come to the clinic immediately if you notice

- Vaginal bleeding or leaking fluid
- Severe headache, blurred vision or sudden swelling of face and hands
- Baby moving much less than usual
- High fever, fits, or severe abdominal pain

## After baby arrives

Postnatal reviews matter just as much: at day 2–3, 6 weeks and beyond, alongside your baby's immunisation schedule at our maternal and child health clinics. Breastfeeding support, family planning and your own recovery are all part of the journey.
